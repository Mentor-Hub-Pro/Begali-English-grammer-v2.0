1. Full Detailed Theory

Learning goal: Spot grammar errors and transform sentences without changing meaning.

  1. Check subject-verb, tense, article, preposition and pronoun before smaller details.
  2. Transformation preserves meaning while changing structure.
  3. Parallel items need parallel grammar forms.
  4. Error spotting works best from large grammar structure to smaller spelling/punctuation details.
  5. Transformation questions test structure, but every correct answer must preserve tense, person and original meaning.
  6. In a tag question, auxiliary choice comes from the statement—not from the final noun alone.

Sentence Correction: 15 error types

Error spotting is not guesswork. Read the sentence in layers: subject–verb, tense, article, preposition, pronoun, word form and unnecessary words.

Subject–Verb Agreement

Find the true subject, then match singular/plural verb.

  1. He go to school. → He goes to school.
  2. They goes to school. → They go to school.
  3. Each students have a pen. → Each student has a pen.
  4. Every boy are ready. → Every boy is ready.
  5. The news are good. → The news is good.
  6. Mathematics are difficult. → Mathematics is difficult.
  7. One of the boys are absent. → One of the boys is absent.
  8. The quality of mangoes are good. → The quality of mangoes is good.
  9. Neither he nor I is ready. → Neither he nor I am ready.
  10. Bread and butter are my breakfast. → Bread and butter is my breakfast.

Tense

Use the time clue and correct verb form.

  1. I have seen him yesterday. → I saw him yesterday.
  2. She did not went. → She did not go.
  3. He has wrote a letter. → He has written a letter.
  4. I am knowing the answer. → I know the answer.
  5. She is living here since 2020. → She has been living here since 2020.
  6. When I arrived, he sleeps. → When I arrived, he was sleeping.
  7. By next year, I complete it. → By next year, I will have completed it.
  8. He go there last week. → He went there last week.
  9. They has finished. → They have finished.
  10. I was reading when he comes. → I was reading when he came.

Article

Use a/an by sound, the for specific nouns, zero article for general use.

  1. He is a honest man. → He is an honest man.
  2. She is an university student. → She is a university student.
  3. The Kolkata is busy. → Kolkata is busy.
  4. He goes to the school daily. → He goes to school daily.
  5. Sun rises in east. → The sun rises in the east.
  6. She is best girl. → She is the best girl.
  7. I like the milk. → I like milk.
  8. He plays flute. → He plays the flute.
  9. I saw elephant. → I saw an elephant.
  10. The Mount Everest is high. → Mount Everest is high.

Preposition

Learn fixed verb/adjective combinations and time/place rules.

  1. He is good in English. → He is good at English.
  2. She is married with him. → She is married to him.
  3. I am waiting you. → I am waiting for you.
  4. He discussed about it. → He discussed it.
  5. We reached to school. → We reached school.
  6. She entered into the room. → She entered the room.
  7. He is senior than me. → He is senior to me.
  8. I prefer tea than coffee. → I prefer tea to coffee.
  9. Meet me in 5 pm. → Meet me at 5 pm.
  10. She was born on 2005. → She was born in 2005.

Pronoun

Check subject/object case, agreement and reference.

  1. Me and Rina went. → Rina and I went.
  2. The teacher called Rina and I. → The teacher called Rina and me.
  3. Between you and I. → Between you and me.
  4. Everyone have a pen. → Everyone has a pen.
  5. Each students must bring their book. → Each student must bring his or her book.
  6. Myself am Rina. → I am Rina.
  7. This is her book; it is her. → This is her book; it is hers.
  8. The boy which came. → The boy who came.
  9. Who did you meet? → Whom did you meet?
  10. One should do their duty. → One should do one’s duty.

Adjective / Adverb

Use adjective after linking verbs and adverb to modify action.

  1. She sings beautiful. → She sings beautifully.
  2. He is feeling badly. → He is feeling bad.
  3. The soup tastes deliciously. → The soup tastes delicious.
  4. He drives careless. → He drives carelessly.
  5. She is a beautifully girl. → She is a beautiful girl.
  6. He works hardly. → He works hard.
  7. I am very happily. → I am very happy.
  8. The flower smells sweetly. → The flower smells sweet.
  9. She speaks fluent English. → She speaks English fluently.
  10. He is real tired. → He is really tired.

Double negative

English standard grammar uses one negative idea, not two.

  1. I do not know nothing. → I do not know anything.
  2. She did not see nobody. → She did not see anybody.
  3. He cannot hardly walk. → He can hardly walk.
  4. I have not no money. → I have no money.
  5. There is not nobody here. → There is nobody here.
  6. She never says nothing. → She never says anything.
  7. We did not go nowhere. → We did not go anywhere.
  8. He has not neither pen nor book. → He has neither pen nor book.
  9. I cannot do nothing. → I cannot do anything.
  10. They did not tell nobody. → They did not tell anybody.

Redundancy

Remove repeated meaning.

  1. Return back soon. → Return soon.
  2. Repeat again. → Repeat.
  3. Free gift. → Gift.
  4. Advance planning. → Planning.
  5. Past history. → History.
  6. Each and every student. → Each student.
  7. He himself personally came. → He personally came.
  8. The reason is because he was ill. → The reason is that he was ill.
  9. Discuss about the issue. → Discuss the issue.
  10. Join together. → Join.

Dangling modifier

The opening phrase must logically describe the subject that follows.

  1. Walking to school, the rain started. → Walking to school, I saw the rain start.
  2. After reading the book, the film was watched. → After reading the book, I watched the film.
  3. To get high marks, hard work is needed. → To get high marks, you need to work hard.
  4. Having finished dinner, the TV was switched on. → Having finished dinner, we switched on the TV.
  5. While crossing the road, a car hit him. → While he was crossing the road, a car hit him.
  6. Looking out of the window, the garden looked lovely. → Looking out of the window, I found the garden lovely.
  7. To improve English, practice is necessary. → To improve English, you must practise.
  8. Running fast, the bus was caught. → Running fast, he caught the bus.
  9. After reaching home, the work was done. → After reaching home, I did the work.
  10. Seeing the snake, fear came. → Seeing the snake, I felt fear.

Parallelism

Keep items in a series in the same grammatical form.

  1. She likes reading, to sing and dancing. → She likes reading, singing and dancing.
  2. He wants to read, writing and to play. → He wants to read, to write and to play.
  3. The job needs patience, to work hard and honesty. → The job needs patience, hard work and honesty.
  4. I enjoy to swim, cycling and to run. → I enjoy swimming, cycling and running.
  5. She is not only clever but also works hard. → She is not only clever but also hardworking.
  6. He likes both to sing and dancing. → He likes both singing and dancing.
  7. The course teaches writing, to speak and reading. → The course teaches writing, speaking and reading.
  8. We should eat well, sleeping enough and exercise. → We should eat well, sleep enough and exercise.
  9. The plan is simple, useful and it saves money. → The plan is simple, useful and economical.
  10. She promised to study, to practise and that she would revise. → She promised to study, practise and revise.

Apostrophe

Use apostrophe for possession or contraction, not ordinary plurals.

  1. The boys bag is new. → The boy’s bag is new.
  2. The girls books are here. → The girls’ books are here.
  3. Its raining. → It’s raining.
  4. The dog wagged it’s tail. → The dog wagged its tail.
  5. Two apple’s. → Two apples.
  6. The teacher’s are ready. → The teachers are ready.
  7. My parents house. → My parents’ house.
  8. Rinas pen. → Rina’s pen.
  9. The childrens toys. → The children’s toys.
  10. Dont worry. → Don’t worry.

Comparative

Use comparative for two and avoid double comparative/superlative.

  1. She is more better. → She is better.
  2. He is tallest than me. → He is taller than me.
  3. This is the most tallest building. → This is the tallest building.
  4. She is junior than me. → She is junior to me.
  5. I prefer tea than coffee. → I prefer tea to coffee.
  6. He is superior than me. → He is superior to me.
  7. This is more preferable. → This is preferable.
  8. No other boy is taller than Rina. → No other boy is as tall as Rina.
  9. He is elder than me. → He is older than me.
  10. The more hard you work, better you score. → The harder you work, the better you score.

Collective noun

Decide whether the group is treated as one unit or individuals; common exam forms have fixed agreement.

  1. The team are winning. → The team is winning.
  2. The police is here. → The police are here.
  3. The cattle is grazing. → The cattle are grazing.
  4. The jury has given their decision. → The jury has given its decision.
  5. A pair of shoes are new. → A pair of shoes is new.
  6. The family are happy. → The family is happy.
  7. The committee have decided. → The committee has decided.
  8. The audience was clapping individually. → The audience were clapping individually.
  9. The staff is arguing among themselves. → The staff are arguing among themselves.
  10. The class are quiet. → The class is quiet.

Number agreement: each/every

Each/every takes a singular noun and singular verb.

  1. Each boys has a pen. → Each boy has a pen.
  2. Every students are ready. → Every student is ready.
  3. Each of the players have a jersey. → Each of the players has a jersey.
  4. Every of the boys is present. → Every boy is present.
  5. Each and every students were called. → Each and every student was called.
  6. Every man and woman are invited. → Every man and woman is invited.
  7. Each of them have a book. → Each of them has a book.
  8. Every one of the answers are correct. → Every one of the answers is correct.
  9. Each person bring their pen. → Each person brings a pen.
  10. Every child have a right. → Every child has a right.

Infinitive vs gerund

Some verbs take to + V1, others take V-ing. Learn the pattern.

  1. He enjoys to sing. → He enjoys singing.
  2. I want going. → I want to go.
  3. She avoided to speak. → She avoided speaking.
  4. They decided going. → They decided to go.
  5. I look forward to meet you. → I look forward to meeting you.
  6. He stopped to smoke. → He stopped smoking.
  7. She made me to cry. → She made me cry.
  8. Let him to go. → Let him go.
  9. It is no use to cry. → It is no use crying.
  10. I am used to get up early. → I am used to getting up early.

Sentence Transformation and Fill in the Blanks

Transformation changes the form, not the meaning. First understand the original sentence, then choose the right pattern.

Simple ↔ Complex ↔ Compound: core examples

Keep the meaning unchanged; alter the link between ideas.

  1. Being ill, he could not come. → As he was ill, he could not come.
  2. As he was ill, he could not come. → He was ill, so he could not come.
  3. He was ill, so he could not come. → Being ill, he could not come.
  4. In spite of poverty, he is honest. → Though he is poor, he is honest.
  5. Though he is poor, he is honest. → He is poor but honest.
  6. He is poor but honest. → In spite of poverty, he is honest.
  7. On seeing the police, the thief ran away. → When the thief saw the police, he ran away.
  8. When the thief saw the police, he ran away. → The thief saw the police and ran away.
  9. The thief saw the police and ran away. → On seeing the police, the thief ran away.
  10. To earn money, he works hard. → He works hard so that he may earn money.
  11. He works hard so that he may earn money. → He works hard and he may earn money.
  12. He works hard and he may earn money. → To earn money, he works hard.
  13. Without working hard, you cannot succeed. → Unless you work hard, you cannot succeed.
  14. Unless you work hard, you cannot succeed. → Work hard or you cannot succeed.
  15. Work hard or you cannot succeed. → Without working hard, you cannot succeed.
  16. The man is too weak to walk. → The man is so weak that he cannot walk.
  17. The man is so weak that he cannot walk. → The man is weak and cannot walk.
  18. The man is weak and cannot walk. → The man is too weak to walk.
  19. Despite rain, we played. → Though it rained, we played.
  20. Though it rained, we played. → It rained but we played.
  21. It rained but we played. → Despite rain, we played.
  22. He saw the train and ran. → Seeing the train, he ran.
  23. Seeing the train, he ran. → When he saw the train, he ran.
  24. When he saw the train, he ran. → He saw the train and ran.
  25. He is rich enough to help. → He is so rich that he can help.
  26. He is so rich that he can help. → He is rich and can help.
  27. He is rich and can help. → He is rich enough to help.
  28. In the event of rain, stay home. → If it rains, stay home.
  29. If it rains, stay home. → It may rain, so stay home.
  30. It may rain, so stay home. → In the event of rain, stay home.

Affirmative → Negative without changing meaning

Use opposite words, double negatives carefully, or structures like “not less than”.

  1. Everyone loves a flower. → There is nobody who does not love a flower.
  2. Only Rina can do it. → None but Rina can do it.
  3. He is always punctual. → He is never late.
  4. As soon as he came, we started. → No sooner had he come than we started.
  5. All men are mortal. → No man is immortal.
  6. Everyone believes him. → Nobody disbelieves him.
  7. He is the best boy. → No other boy is as good as he.
  8. I shall always remember you. → I shall never forget you.
  9. He is too weak to walk. → He is not strong enough to walk.
  10. She is honest. → She is not dishonest.
  11. The boy is very clever. → The boy is not dull.
  12. I have many friends. → I am not without friends.
  13. He is rich. → He is not poor.
  14. She is present. → She is not absent.
  15. The room is full. → The room is not empty.

Exclamatory ↔ Assertive

Replace what/how with very/great and use an assertive statement.

  1. What a beautiful flower! → It is a very beautiful flower.
  2. How happy I am! → I am very happy.
  3. What a pity! → It is a great pity.
  4. How foolish I was! → I was very foolish.
  5. What a lovely day! → It is a very lovely day.
  6. How fast he runs! → He runs very fast.
  7. What a dangerous road! → It is a very dangerous road.
  8. How sweet the song is! → The song is very sweet.
  9. What a brave boy he is! → He is a very brave boy.
  10. How cold it is! → It is very cold.
  11. It is a very beautiful view. → What a beautiful view it is!
  12. She is very kind. → How kind she is!
  13. It is a great surprise. → What a surprise it is!
  14. The child is very clever. → How clever the child is!
  15. It is a very sad story. → What a sad story it is!

Degree transformations

Use No other / Very few / one of the with correct degree.

  1. Rina is the tallest girl in the class. → No other girl in the class is as tall as Rina.
  2. Rina is the tallest girl in the class. → Rina is taller than any other girl in the class.
  3. Very few metals are as useful as iron. → Iron is one of the most useful metals.
  4. No other city is as busy as Kolkata. → Kolkata is busier than any other city.
  5. Mount Everest is the highest peak. → No other peak is as high as Mount Everest.
  6. Gold is more precious than most metals. → Very few metals are as precious as gold.
  7. This is one of the best books. → Very few books are as good as this.
  8. Rina is wiser than any other girl. → No other girl is as wise as Rina.
  9. The Ganga is one of the longest rivers. → Very few rivers are as long as the Ganga.
  10. He is the strongest boy. → No other boy is as strong as he.
  11. Very few animals are as faithful as dogs. → Dogs are among the most faithful animals.
  12. No other poet is as famous as Tagore. → Tagore is more famous than any other poet.
  13. This road is shorter than any other road. → No other road is as short as this road.
  14. Rina is one of the most diligent students. → Very few students are as diligent as Rina.
  15. The Nile is longer than most rivers. → Very few rivers are as long as the Nile.

Too…to / So…that / Such…that

Too + adjective + to; so + adjective/adverb + that; such + adjective + noun + that.

  1. He is too weak to walk. → He is so weak that he cannot walk.
  2. He is too weak to walk. → He is such a weak man that he cannot walk.
  3. The box is too heavy to lift. → The box is so heavy that I cannot lift it.
  4. It was too dark to see. → It was so dark that we could not see.
  5. She is too proud to beg. → She is so proud that she cannot beg.
  6. The tea is too hot to drink. → The tea is so hot that I cannot drink it.
  7. He ran too fast to catch. → He ran so fast that I could not catch him.
  8. The boy is too young to vote. → The boy is so young that he cannot vote.
  9. It is too late to start. → It is so late that we cannot start.
  10. The puzzle is too difficult to solve. → The puzzle is so difficult that we cannot solve it.
  11. It was such a hot day that we stayed indoors. → The day was so hot that we stayed indoors.
  12. She is such a clever girl that she solves it. → She is so clever that she solves it.
  13. It was such heavy rain that we stopped. → It rained so heavily that we stopped.
  14. He is such a kind man that all love him. → He is so kind that all love him.
  15. It was such a beautiful scene that we stopped. → The scene was so beautiful that we stopped.

Combining sentences

Choose the connector based on person, thing, purpose, condition, time or contrast.

  1. I know the boy. He won the prize. → I know the boy who won the prize.
  2. This is the book. I bought it. → This is the book which I bought.
  3. The pen is mine. It is on the table. → The pen which is on the table is mine.
  4. He works hard. He may succeed. → He works hard so that he may succeed.
  5. She studies daily. She wants to pass. → She studies daily in order to pass.
  6. Work hard. You will not succeed otherwise. → Unless you work hard, you will not succeed.
  7. Wait here. I return. → Wait here until I return.
  8. He was poor. He was honest. → Though he was poor, he was honest.
  9. The rain stopped. We started. → We started after the rain stopped.
  10. The bell rang. The class began. → The class began when the bell rang.
  11. The man is my uncle. He is wearing a cap. → The man who is wearing a cap is my uncle.
  12. I need a pen. It writes smoothly. → I need a pen that writes smoothly.
  13. She saved money. She could buy a book. → She saved money so that she could buy a book.
  14. Do not leave. I come back. → Do not leave until I come back.
  15. He was tired. He continued working. → Though he was tired, he continued working.
