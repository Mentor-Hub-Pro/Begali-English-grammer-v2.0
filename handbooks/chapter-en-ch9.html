1. Full Detailed Theory

Learning goal: Master plural patterns, gender labels and degree transformations.

  1. Plural rules depend on ending and word type; irregular forms must be memorised.
  2. Common gender can refer to any person; neuter refers to things/ideas.
  3. Positive, comparative and superlative compare zero, two and more than two.
  4. Some plural-looking nouns are singular in meaning (news, mathematics), while some tools are plural (scissors, trousers).
  5. Degree transformations require the same comparison group; “any other” avoids comparing a person with itself.
  6. Modern English often prefers gender-neutral common nouns, but traditional pair forms still appear in school grammar questions.

Number: singular and plural — 12 rules

Degree: positive, comparative and superlative

Positive states a quality, comparative compares two, and superlative compares more than two. The structure must change with the degree.

15 degree transformations

Keep the group of comparison the same.

  1. Rina is the tallest girl in the class. → No other girl in the class is as tall as Rina.
  2. Rina is the tallest girl in the class. → Rina is taller than any other girl in the class.
  3. No other metal is as useful as iron. → Iron is more useful than any other metal.
  4. No other metal is as useful as iron. → Iron is the most useful metal.
  5. Very few cities are as busy as Kolkata. → Kolkata is one of the busiest cities.
  6. Kolkata is one of the busiest cities. → Very few cities are as busy as Kolkata.
  7. The Ganga is longer than most rivers. → Very few rivers are as long as the Ganga.
  8. Very few rivers are as long as the Ganga. → The Ganga is one of the longest rivers.
  9. Gold is more precious than most metals. → Very few metals are as precious as gold.
  10. No other boy is as strong as Raju. → Raju is stronger than any other boy.
  11. Raju is stronger than any other boy. → Raju is the strongest boy.
  12. This is one of the best books. → Very few books are as good as this.
  13. Very few students are as diligent as Mina. → Mina is one of the most diligent students.
  14. Mount Everest is the highest peak. → No other peak is as high as Mount Everest.
  15. No other poet is as famous as Tagore. → Tagore is more famous than any other poet.

No other / Very few structures

No other = one clearly highest; Very few = one among a small top group.

  1. No other bird is as fast as the eagle.
  2. No other river is as long as the Nile.
  3. No other player is as skilled as Rina.
  4. No other building is as tall as this.
  5. No other flower is as beautiful as the rose.
  6. No other month is as cold as January.
  7. No other animal is as faithful as the dog.
  8. No other city is as old as Rome.
  9. No other metal is as valuable as gold.
  10. No other hill is as high as Everest.
  11. Very few animals are as intelligent as dolphins.
  12. Very few books are as useful as this one.
  13. Very few girls are as kind as Mina.
  14. Very few roads are as wide as this road.
  15. Very few games are as popular as football.
  16. Very few students are as punctual as Rina.
  17. Very few rivers are as deep as this river.
  18. Very few flowers are as fragrant as jasmine.
  19. Very few machines are as useful as computers.
  20. Very few cities are as crowded as Kolkata.
Shortcut: Do not write “more better” or “most tallest.” Use only one comparison marker: better, tallest, more useful, most useful.
Memory trick: No other = as…as; than any other = comparative class; the best/most = superlative pass.
Common mistakes:
  • ✗ She is more better. → ✓ She is better.
  • ✗ He is the most tallest. → ✓ He is the tallest.
  • ✗ Rina is taller than any girl. → ✓ Rina is taller than any other girl.
  • ✗ No other boy is taller than Rina. → ✓ No other boy is as tall as Rina.
  • ✗ He is junior than me. → ✓ He is junior to me.
Exam focus:
  • Positive of “Rina is the tallest girl” → No other girl is as tall as Rina.
  • Comparative of “No other metal is as useful as iron” → Iron is more useful than any other metal..
  • Choose correct: better, not more better.
