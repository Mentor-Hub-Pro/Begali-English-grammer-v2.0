1. Full Detailed Theory

Learning goal: Change active sentences to passive without changing tense or meaning.

  1. Object becomes passive subject; use tense-appropriate be + V3.
  2. Pronoun changes: I→me, he→him, they→them after by.
  3. Intransitive verbs have no normal passive because they have no object.
  4. Passive voice changes focus, not basic meaning; use it when receiver/action matters more than doer.
  5. The main verb must be V3 in passive; tense lives in the auxiliary be.
  6. Two-object verbs permit two passive forms, but use to before the indirect object when the direct object becomes subject.

Voice: Active and Passive

In active voice, the subject does the action. In passive voice, the object receives the action. Object becomes subject; choose the correct form of be + V3.

Simple Present

am/is/are + V3

  1. People grow rice. → Rice is grown.
  2. She writes a letter. → A letter is written by her.
  3. They clean the room. → The room is cleaned.
  4. We play football. → Football is played by us.
  5. The teacher teaches English. → English is taught by the teacher.
  6. He opens the door. → The door is opened by him.
  7. Mother cooks food. → Food is cooked by mother.
  8. Students answer questions. → Questions are answered by students.

Imperatives, two objects and by-agent

Some voice questions need a special pattern. Imperatives use Let + object + be + V3. With two objects, either object may become the passive subject.

Passive of imperatives: 10 examples

Use Let + object + be + V3. For negative commands use Let not + object + be + V3.

  1. Open the door. → Let the door be opened.
  2. Write the answer. → Let the answer be written.
  3. Do the work. → Let the work be done.
  4. Clean the room. → Let the room be cleaned.
  5. Read the lesson. → Let the lesson be read.
  6. Do not waste time. → Let not time be wasted.
  7. Do not tell a lie. → Let not a lie be told.
  8. Keep the door closed. → Let the door be kept closed.
  9. Follow the rules. → Let the rules be followed.
  10. Help the poor. → Let the poor be helped.

Passive of SVOO sentences: 10 examples

With give, tell, show, teach, offer, send etc., make either indirect object or direct object the subject.

  1. He gave me a pen. → I was given a pen.
  2. He gave me a pen. → A pen was given to me.
  3. The teacher taught us English. → We were taught English.
  4. The teacher taught us English. → English was taught to us.
  5. Mother told me a story. → I was told a story.
  6. Mother told me a story. → A story was told to me.
  7. They offered her a job. → She was offered a job.
  8. They sent us a message. → We were sent a message.
  9. Father bought me a bicycle. → I was bought a bicycle.
  10. The guide showed us the way. → We were shown the way.

When passive is impossible

Intransitive verbs do not take an object, so a normal passive cannot be made.

  1. He sleeps. → no passive
  2. The baby cries. → no passive
  3. Birds fly. → no passive
  4. The sun rises. → no passive
  5. She arrived late. → no passive
  6. They laughed loudly. → no passive
  7. The child fell. → no passive
  8. The train departed. → no passive
  9. We walked home. → no passive
  10. The river flows. → no passive
  11. He died yesterday. → no passive
  12. She smiled. → no passive
  13. The dog barked. → no passive
  14. Leaves fell. → no passive
  15. The audience clapped. → no passive

Use or drop the by-agent

Keep by + agent when it is important, unknown, surprising or needed for clarity. Drop it when the doer is obvious or unimportant.

  1. The poem was written by Rabindranath. (keep)
  2. The thief was arrested by the police. (often keep)
  3. The road was repaired. (drop unknown/unimportant agent)
  4. English is spoken in many countries. (drop general people)
  5. The Taj Mahal was built by Shah Jahan. (keep)
  6. The window was broken. (agent unknown)
  7. The patient was examined by the doctor. (keep if relevant)
  8. Rice is grown in this region. (drop general farmers)
  9. The match was won by our team. (keep)
  10. The classroom was cleaned. (drop)
Shortcut: Never force a by-agent. “The road was repaired by someone” is weaker than “The road was repaired” unless the person matters.
Memory trick: Object না থাকলে passive নয়; দুই object হলে দুই পথ হয়।

  • ✗ I was given a pen by he. → ✓ I was given a pen by him.
  • ✗ The baby was slept. → ✓ The baby slept.
  • ✗ A pen was given me. → ✓ A pen was given to me.
  • ✗ Let not the rules broken. → ✓ Let not the rules be broken.
Exam focus:
  • Imperative passive: “Close the gate.” → Let the gate be closed..
  • SVOO passive: “She gave me a gift.” → I was given a gift..

2. Memory Tricks, Shortcuts & Exam Traps

Shortcut: Object first → be in same tense → V3 → by-agent only if useful.
Exam trap: “He sleeps” cannot become “He is slept”; sleep is intransitive.
High-priority focus: Tense-wise passive formula, pronoun change, imperatives, SVOO and intransitive verb limitation.
Study flow: Find object → Choose be form → Use V3 → Check pronoun and agent
